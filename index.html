1. The Goal (Plain English)

Plain-English Overview · All 6 IMBA Teammates

Should an investor or advisor in Asia pay higher active fund fees, or simply hold VOO?

The Decision-Maker: An investor or financial advisor in Asia deciding whether to pay ongoing fees for actively managed US mutual funds or simply hold VOO (Vanguard's S&P 500 ETF, 0.03% expense ratio), plus whether balanced, bond, and liquid-alternative funds meaningfully diversify an S&P 500 holding. All 13 funds are evaluated on the exact same 144-month window (November 2014 – October 2026), set by the full history of our youngest fund (QMNIX).

Part A · RQ1 (7 Active Equity Funds)

Did any active equity fund earn statistically significant, fee-net alpha above zero vs. VOO?

We test the null hypothesis H₀: α = 0 using CAPM regressions with Newey-West standard errors. An interval that includes zero means no statistical evidence of outperformance.

AGTHX · AWSHX · FCNTX · DODGX · SEEGX · FDGRX · VPMAX
Part B · RQ2 (6 Diversifying Funds)

Do balanced, income, bond, and liquid-alternative funds meaningfully diversify VOO?

Evaluated by lower beta, lower annualized volatility, and smaller maximum drawdowns vs. VOO—not by beating the S&P 500 (alpha is shown for context only).

ABALX · AMECX · PIMIX · AQMIX · QMNIX · QAI

Are Fees Included in Our Study?

Yes. Yahoo Finance monthly dividend-adjusted returns are already net of ongoing annual expense ratios (e.g., ~0.65%–1.00% for active funds vs. 0.03% for VOO). Front-end sales loads (which apply to AGTHX and AWSHX) and taxes are excluded, which actually makes our test generous to the active funds.

Why Isn't US Treasury (TB3MS) in the Title?

FRED TB3MS (3-month US Treasury bill, converted to a monthly rate) is our measuring ruler inside the CAPM and Sharpe ratio formulas to compute excess returns—not an investment product we are evaluating or recommending. It belongs in Methodology, not the headline.

Plain-English Finance Cheat Sheet

Every quantitative metric in our project explained clearly for non-finance IMBA teammates.

1. Annualized Return (CAGR)

How fast $1 compounds each year

Computed from monthly returns as (End ÷ Start)^(12 ÷ 144) − 1 and displayed visually with a growth-of-$1 chart vs. VOO.

2. Volatility & Max Drawdown

How bumpy the ride & worst crash

Annualized Volatility is monthly SD × √12. Max Drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough percentage drop an investor actually lived through.

3. Sharpe Ratio

Reward earned per unit of total risk

Average monthly excess return above safe cash (TB3MS) divided by its standard deviation, × √12. Higher means better return per unit of stress.

4. Beta (Market Exposure)

How much a fund moves with VOO

The slope of our CAPM regression (with 95% CI). Beta ≈ 1.0 moves 1-for-1 with VOO; low beta signals strong diversification for Part B.

5. Alpha & 95% Confidence Interval

Return beyond market exposure

Annualized CAPM intercept (monthly × 12). If the 95% CI includes zero, there is no statistical evidence of outperformance.

6. Newey-West SEs & Power

Protecting intervals & reading limits

Newey-West SEs correct for autocorrelation/heteroskedasticity in monthly returns. With 144 months, power is limited: "not significant" means "no evidence," not "zero skill."

Managerial Decision Rule

Default to VOO unless a Part A active equity fund's annualized alpha has a 95% confidence interval entirely above zero—and even then treat it as a flag, not proof, because testing 7 funds creates up to a ~30% chance of at least one false positive at the 5% level. Judge Part B funds by beta and drawdown protection, never by beating VOO.

5. Risks & Mistakes to Avoid

6 Traps

Analytical & Finance Traps

1. Misreading Non-Significant Alpha as "Zero Skill"

Pair 3: Xiao Ai & Anne

The Risk: Claiming a non-significant alpha proves managers have no skill, ignoring limited 144-month statistical power.

How We Prevent It: Always state "no statistical evidence of outperformance" and interpret the width of each 95% CI.

2. Multiple-Testing False Flags

Pair 1 & Pair 3

The Risk: Treating a single p < 0.05 result across 7 Part A funds as proof of skill despite up to a ~30% chance of at least one false positive.

How We Prevent It: Embed the multiple-testing warning directly into our Decision Rule—treat any positive CI as a flag, not proof.

3. Missing Newey-West SEs or TB3MS Mismatch

Pair 1: Drew & Phoebe

The Risk: Using plain OLS standard errors (falsely narrow CIs) or forgetting to convert annualized FRED TB3MS to a monthly rate.

How We Prevent It: Pair 1 verifies monthly TB3MS conversion and Newey-West lags in code, backed by Xiao Ai's Nov 28 sanity check.

4. Judging Part B Diversifiers by S&P 500 Alpha

Pair 2: Stefanie & Alice

The Risk: Evaluating balanced, bond, and liquid-alternative funds on whether they beat VOO, which misstates their role.

How We Prevent It: Lead Part B with beta, annualized volatility, and maximum drawdown vs. VOO; label alpha as context only.

5. Ignoring Biases, Shared Families & Tech Bets

Pair 2: Stefanie & Alice

The Risk: Omitting survivorship/2026-size bias, Capital Group/Fidelity parent overlap, mega-cap tech bets (NVIDIA ~16% in FDGRX, ~12% in SEEGX), front-end loads, or $1M–$5M minimums.

How We Prevent It: Pair 2 builds a dedicated Limitations & Frictions audit table matched directly to our proposal.

6. Unverified AI Code or Missing AI Appendix

All Pairs · Audited by Pair 2

The Risk: Submitting unchecked AI outputs ("the AI said so" is never a defense) or omitting the mandatory AI-Use Statement & Prompt Appendix.

How We Prevent It: Pair 1 & Pair 3 verify all code outputs; Pair 2 maintains the team AI-Use Statement & Prompt Appendix from Day 1.
